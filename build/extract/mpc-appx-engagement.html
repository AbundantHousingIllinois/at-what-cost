Appendix: Engagement methodology

The Assessment was designed as a stakeholder-guided research project. MPC gathered a wide range of decision-makers, thought leaders, and community organizations to learn and problem-solve together. This appendix provides more detail on the engagement conducted over the course of the Assessment. It includes an engagement timeline, example activities MPC used to share information and seek feedback, and a discussion of how MPC documented and synthesized feedback.

Vision Statement created by Community Advisory Group to guide development of zoning resource

“We want to see access to nature in our neighborhoods—trees and plants, an open, public riverfront, parks, and healthy air. We want to walk down safe and accessible streets that are friendly for pedestrians, bicyclists, children, and people of all ages and abilities. We want the places we live and work to feel welcoming and active, with affordable housing options that prevent displacement, do not compete with sustainability needs, and neighborhoods of a sufficient density to avoid sprawl. We want consistent and reliable transit access, fresh food and clean water, cultural events, schools, and a variety of small businesses that serve the community. We aspire to have people of all ages spending time out and about, in clean spaces filled with uses that meet community needs. Finally, we envision a community that is resilient and adaptive to changing climate, social, and economic conditions.”

Engagement timeline

  • ADVISORY GROUPS
  • ADDITIONAL ENGAGEMENT
  • PUBLIC EVENTS
  1. FALL 2022
    RECRUITMENT OF STEERING COMMITTEE MEMBERS

    MPC identified and recruited a mix of stakeholders to make up the Zoning Assessment’s Steering Committee, including representatives from government, civic and community groups, the private sector, and academia to participate in regular meetings to guide the project.

  2. JANUARY 2023 – NOVEMBER 2024
    STEERING COMMITTEE MEETINGS

    MPC engaged 42 members of the Steering Committee in 14 meetings. The Steering Committee:

    • Developed and agreed on key definitions of equity, sustainability, and public health
    • Brainstormed a list of zoning process challenges and successes
    • Brainstormed a list of more than 120 outcomes of interest for the Assessment
    • Selected 13 priority outcomes through consensus voting and discussion:
    • Reviewed initial drafts of the research findings
    • Provided feedback on policy implications of the research findings
  3. SUMMER 2022 – WINTER 2025
    FOCUS GROUPS AND SMALL-GROUP CONVERSATIONS

    MPC engaged 51 participants in focus group conversations about zoning and land use. Focus group participants:

    • Contributed to a list of zoning challenges and successes
    • Reviewed and discussed the research findings
    • Provided technical advice on policy ideas and recommendations
  4. SUMMER 2022 YEAR – WINTER 2025
    ONE-ON-ONE CONVERSATIONS

    MPC engaged more than 50 people in direct conversations. In these conversations, we:

    • Held individual conversations about zoning and land use challenges and successes
    • Learned from experts who had undertaken similar projects and initiatives
    • Learned from policymakers, including aldermanic offices, about how they approached zoning and land use
  5. FALL 2023 – WINTER 2023
    RECRUITMENT OF COMMUNITY ADVISORY GROUP MEMBERS

    MPC recruited representatives from civic and neighborhood groups across the city to support the creation of a zoning resource (zine) for residents and community groups.

  6. FEBRUARY 2024 – MARCH 2025
    COMMUNITY ADVISORY GROUP MEETINGS

    MPC engaged 36 members of the Community Advisory Group in 7 meetings. The group:

    • Co-created and guided the development of a public resource (zine) for understanding and engaging with zoning, guided by a shared vision statement
    • Reviewed the research findings
    • Provided feedback on policy ideas and recommendations
  7. JULY 2024 – SEPTEMBER 2025
    ZONING ASSESSMENT WORKSHOPS
    • Over the course of 4 in-person, half-day workshops, MPC engaged stakeholders on the research and recommendations for the Assessment.
    • One of these workshops included a panel discussion, “Untangling zoning impacts and promoting equity through reform,” featuring national experts on zoning and land use. Morgan Malone of City Bureau moderated a panel with Sara Bronin of the National Zoning Atlas, Nolan Gray, author of “Arbitrary Lines: how Zoning Broke the American City and How to Fix It,” and the Assessment’s research partner Yonah Freemark, Director of Urban Institute’s Land Use Lab.
  8. JULY – NOVEMBER 2025
    YOUTH ZONING WORKSHOPS

    MPC held 2 workshops with youth interested in zoning and land use topics through Palenque LSNA and the Teen Coalition for Chinatown. Participants learned about zoning and land use basics and discussed research findings and recommendations from the Assessment.

  9. AUGUST 2025
    DRAWN TO ACTION: A ZINE LAUNCH AND PUBLIC CONVERSATION ON ART AND DESIGN FOR CIVIC ENGAGEMENT

    MPC held an event at the Chicago Architecture Center to celebrate the launch of our zine, “In the Zone: A Chicagoan’s Guide to Zoning and Land Use.” The event featured a live illustrator and panel discussion between our zine’s illustrator Lucie Van der Elst, artist Tonika Lewis Johnson, Duo’s Carlos Robles-Shanahan, and the Architecture Center’s Angela Esposito.

  10. JANUARY – MAY 2026
    RECOMMENDATIONS REFINEMENT STAKEHOLDER CONVERSATIONS

    MPC staff re-connected with stakeholders in one-on-one conversations to clarify and strengthen the Assessment recommendations and surface considerations about implementation before finalizing.

  11. JULY 2026 - ONGOING
    ADDITIONAL ENGAGEMENT: ZONING 101 WORKSHOPS

    Through partnerships with local library branches and community groups, MPC is holding free workshops for the public on the basics of zoning and land use and how to engage with development decisions in their communities.

Documenting and synthesizing feedback on recommendations

In workshops between 2024 and 2025, MPC collected more than 1,200 pieces of feedback from stakeholders on an initial set of zoning reform recommendations, which were used to update the recommendations to become the revised set that eventually appeared in this report.

In this process, MPC documented each individual piece of feedback received—whether through interaction on an activity board, notes in a workshop workbook, or other feedback channels—in a central spreadsheet for each of the project’s priority outcomes (with the exception of Productive Land Use, which did not have a set of proposed recommendations). Alongside this qualitative feedback on the draft recommendations, voting was used to gauge levels of agreement among stakeholders with each individual recommendation, and votes were also individually tracked and summarized.

MPC staff reviewed and coded the feedback to summarize the themes and issues that emerged most frequently, in these categories: (1) This could be effective because (2) This could be improved by (3) If this was implemented, things to consider (4) What is missing. These themes were used by teams of MPC staff, alongside the voting results, to update recommendations.
