[B 49,189,565,249]
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}In addition to allowing more housing near transit (see {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #0000ff}page 16{ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}), MPC recommends adjusting zoning near transit to support the 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}development of mixed-use employment hubs and business corridors, especially in areas with fewer jobs and higher vacancy rates 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}where minimizing barriers to development is one step towards revitalization. This may require reevaluating business and commercial 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}zoning district categories to ensure that districts are flexible enough, allowing both residential uses and a wide enough range of 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}commercial uses.

[B 73,258,561,282]
  {ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #755134}Expand pedestrian streets near transit: {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #755134}Standardize the use of the pedestrian street designation near transit and modernize 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #755134}pedestrian street eligibility and standards. 
[B 49,291,566,339]
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Currently the “pedestrian street” designation is a limited, preservation-oriented tool, allowing individual alders to opt-in to pedestrian 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}friendliness standards for specific, eligible streets. This treats pedestrian friendliness as an exceptional case requiring additional 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}legislation and approvals, while streetscapes that are hostile to pedestrians are considered the status quo. Our current approach has 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}had inequitable outcomes, with pedestrian streets concentrated on the North side. 
[B 49,348,556,420]
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}MPC recommends standardizing the proactive use of the pedestrian street designation on business corridors near transit citywide, 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}while making allowances for preexisting uses and exceptional situations, like transit along highways. MPC also recommends 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}strengthening the definition of the pedestrian street designation itself. This means including a list of prohibited and explicitly 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}allowed uses in the definition, limiting future downzonings, and updating eligibility criteria to allow pedestrian streets to be used 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}as a forward-looking planning tool citywide. A standardized, expanded set of pedestrian streets could also be anchors for citywide 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}planning efforts discussed on {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #0000ff}page 31

  {ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #755134}Bring small-scale business into neighborhoods: {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #755134}Increase the types of businesses allowed in residential neighborhoods
[B 49,450,560,510]
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Currently, only a very limited number of businesses—such as bed and breakfasts and vacation rentals—are allowed in 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}residential districts (and even then, only in some districts). “Business Live/Work Units” and “Artist Live/Work Units” are only allowed 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}in areas zoned for business and commercial use, often also requiring approval by the Zoning Board of Appeals. Allowing small-
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}scale, low-impact businesses like corner stores, cafes, small-scale retail, and artist studios is a step toward improving the livability of 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}neighborhoods and supporting small business development.
