  {ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #755134}Zoning for “pedestrian streets,” areas designed as walkable business corridors, is concentrated on the North side. 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Pedestrian streets are streets designated in the zoning code that must follow certain pedestrian-friendly street design 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}standards. Making a street an official pedestrian street currently requires a zoning change, in which individual alders opt in a specific 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}street in their ward. Importantly, only some business corridors—those with high concentrations of stores and restaurants, and with 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}low vacancy—are currently eligible for the designation. Pedestrian streets are concentrated on the North side. Of Chicago’s 62 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}pedestrian streets outside of downtown, only 12 are not on the North side.
