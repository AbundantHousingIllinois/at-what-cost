[B 49,148,566,233]
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}The Planned Developments process was originally designed for larger, more complex developments like medical campuses, but the 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}process is currently used for most new multi-family development in Chicago. In addition to other recommendations in this report that 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}make it easier to build multi-family housing through the zoning code, increasing the thresholds that trigger the Planned Development 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}process can help ensure Planned Development review is reserved for development types that require more review (such as certain 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}manufacturing types, development on or near waterfronts, large institutions, or other sensitive development types). Apartment 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}buildings, on the other hand, expand access to housing, do not reduce local home values, and fit into Chicago’s existing built form. 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}As such, with minor exceptions, they should not undergo special review. For more on Planned Developments, see {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #0000ff}page 17

[B 250,274,565,347]
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Even if we move towards affordability by addressing our supply challenges, 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}market-rate housing will not meet the need for deeply affordable housing. 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Zoning changes that make room for more homes should be paired with policies 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}that prioritize and incentivize the creation and preservation of homes affordable 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}to the lowest-income Chicagoans. Though zoning cannot achieve this goal on its 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}own, it is one part of a larger strategy.
[B 250,355,557,428]
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}In addition to maintaining the city’s Affordable Requirements Ordinance, which 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}requires certain multifamily development to include a set-aside number of 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}affordable units, MPC recommends updating zoning policies to further support 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}the creation of new income-limited affordable housing units. This could mean 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}fast-tracking zoning approvals for affordable developments, and prohibiting 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}downzoning in reaction to proposed affordable developments. 
[B 49,436,564,533]
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Promoting affordability also means taking active steps to preserve existing affordable units. This requires funding to support 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}acquisition and rehabilitation of properties. Without this funding, preservation strategies will be limited in their effectiveness. 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Nevertheless, zoning and land use policies can play a role: for example, expanding and strengthening the existing “Predominance of 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}the Block” approach—which uses the housing already on a block to determine a context-appropriate density floor for development—
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}could help protect against deconversion of multifamily housing, which tends to be more affordable, into single-family homes, 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}which tend to be more expensive. This approach is currently only used in Chicago within designated geographic boundaries. 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Other protections could also be explored to specifically target local conditions in areas experiencing ongoing disinvestment or 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}gentrification pressures.
