  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Navigating zoning in Chicago can be extremely complex. There are at least 67 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}zoning districts,{ProximaNova-Regular 5.2 #000000}16{ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000} with at least 28 geographic overlays layered on top of these 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}districts.{ProximaNova-Regular 5.2 #000000} 17{ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000} At least 180 specific land use types are listed in the zoning code, and 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}at least five government decision-making bodies{ProximaNova-Regular 5.2 #000000}18{ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000} make official decisions about 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}zoning. Contingencies and overlay geographies written into the code can make 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}identifying specific requirements, like setbacks and other bulk and density 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}requirements, a mazelike task. Adding to all this complexity is the reality of 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}aldermanic control over zoning decisions, which takes a variety of forms across 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}wards, as well as the increasingly common use of Planned Developments as a 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}development vehicle (see {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #0000ff}page 17{ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}). 
[B 49,256,563,304]
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}For a resident interested in understanding how zoning impacts their neighborhood, excess complexity can make city government 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}inaccessible, which erodes trust. And when complexity becomes standard, even for types of development that meet citywide goals, 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}the result is added costs and delays, creating an unpredictable development environment. Those with the resources to navigate the 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}system and weather delays—such as land use attorneys and/or surplus financing—are the ones who benefit.
