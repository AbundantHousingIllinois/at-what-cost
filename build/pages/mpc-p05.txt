  {ProximaNova-Bold 9.0 #000000}Zoning reform is not a silver bullet. There are generational challenges ahead. {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Many factors beyond zoning shape our 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}communities, such as public and private investment and disinvestment, community organizations, market incentives, and tax 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}structures. Zoning changes that make room for more homes must be paired with policies that prioritize and incentivize the creation 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}and preservation of homes affordable to the lowest-income Chicagoans. Meanwhile, the unusually large size of City Council for 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}the size of our population, our lack of at-large council members, and our lack of a city charter all fuel our culture of unchecked 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}aldermanic prerogative. And with our piecemeal approach to planning, we lack a way to bring in many voices to conversations about 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}development without getting sidetracked by hyper-local opposition, or piling all of a community’s legitimate needs onto a single 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}development. Zoning reform alone will not solve our city’s fundamental and interrelated problems of resource allocation  
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}and governance.

  {ProximaNova-Bold 9.0 #000000}Chicago.{ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000} We can reimagine our policies—zoning and beyond—to build a place where the inequitable outcomes we see reflected in

  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}have access to transportation that connects them; where vibrant, livable communities replace empty lots and small businesses can

  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}collaborative and forward-looking status quo that empowers Chicagoans—not ward-by-ward, but across the city.
