  {ProximaNova-Bold 11.0 #000000}Longitudinal zoning data
[B 49,596,566,717]
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}The keystone underlying the research findings in the Assessment was a longitudinal zoning dataset we created for Chicago. This 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}dataset allowed us to understand how zoning changes over time may or may not have had an impact on each of the different priority 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}outcomes. To create this dataset, we collected zoning district shapefiles across three years: 2003, 2012, and 2023. We collected 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}the accompanying zoning ordinances for those years so we could quantify bulk and density requirements, use tables, and other 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}relevant zoning information associated with each zoning district and append them to the zoning district shapefiles. We also collected 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}three different land use shapefiles from the Chicago Metropolitan Agency for Planning (CMAP) to understand how actual land uses 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}changed over time, in addition to zoning. Land use data closely, though not perfectly, matched zoning ordinance years: 2001, 2010, 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}and 2020. Zoning and land use variables were used as the primary independent variables across all outcomes. For our analysis of 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}downzoning’s historical impacts, we worked with George Kisiel, whose data on parcels that had been downzoned over the course of 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}several decades was invaluable in enabling us to examine outcomes.
