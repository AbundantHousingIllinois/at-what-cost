  {ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #6a2310}The special use process is a barrier for small 
  {ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #6a2310}businesses.{ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000} Most special use applications (78%) come 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}from business-oriented (B and C) zoning districts. These are 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}applications for a range of uses, including beauty salons, tattoo 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}shops, outdoor patios, and community centers. Guided by 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}recommendations provided by the Department of Planning and 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Development, the ZBA reviews an average of approximately 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}230 special use applications every year. Despite improvements 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}to the application in recent years, navigating the ZBA is a 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}complex, lengthy process, often requiring the help of an 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}attorney. The opacity of the process and lack of consistent 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}guidelines for the ZBA end up favoring applicants with time and resources to navigate the process, creating a pay-to-play system. 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}And although officially, alders are not part of the ZBA decision-making process, in practice, alders hold major sway in the outcome of 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}a ZBA application, so these applications often require political support to move forward. Additional costs and delays can stall or 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}shutter a small business that’s otherwise ready to open. The outcome is a system that favors applicants with greater access to 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}resources.

  {ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #6a2310}Data about zoning decisions is not generally released to the public in a standardized, searchable format. {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}In the case of 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}ZBA meetings and Planned Development agreements, information is released in the form of individual meeting-by-meeting or 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}project-by-project PDFs. In both of these cases, in order to better understand these processes, MPC needed to compile unique 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}datasets manually by pulling information from hundreds of PDFs. No publicly searchable dataset exists, which could be helpful for a 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}potential small business owner to find examples of previous special use applications. In the case of administrative adjustments, no 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}information is released publicly at all. Meanwhile, zoning amendments occur in the form of individual ordinances passed through City 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Council. These are accessible to those who know how to navigate the City Clerk’s website, but there is no public dataset that would 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}allow for a systematic review of zoning amendment applications, which could have important implications for zoning policy.
