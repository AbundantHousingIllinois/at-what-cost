  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}As most households’ largest monthly expense, the cost of 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}housing affects everyone.{ProximaNova-Regular 5.2 #000000} 3{ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000} MPC and the Urban Institute’s 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}research found that the problem is worsening: housing has 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}become significantly less affordable over the last 20 years, 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}particularly for Black and Latinx residents. One key explanation is 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}that we do not have enough housing in Chicago to meet 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}demand, and we do not have enough affordable housing: the 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Chicago metro area is short 230,000 affordable rental homes for 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}very low-income renter households.{ProximaNova-Regular 5.2 #000000} 4{ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000} But Chicago’s zoning code 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}limits how much new housing can legally be built: it is illegal to 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}build new multifamily housing on most of the city’s residential 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}land without a zoning change, and most new multi-family housing 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}gets built as Planned Developments—a process that adds time, 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}money, and complication for developers. Our work also confirms 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}what research{ProximaNova-Regular 5.2 #000000}5{ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000} on downzoning and historic preservation in 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Chicago has shown—that these tools have functioned to create 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}more exclusionary neighborhoods over time.
