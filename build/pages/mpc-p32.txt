  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}In Chicago, our efforts to update zoning tend to be piecemeal—and always at the discretion of the local alder—rather than guided by 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}citywide planning and citywide goals. 
[IMAGE 49,166,62,180]
[B 49,163,560,248]
  {ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #4f7a60}Chicago does not regularly engage in comprehensive citywide planning.{ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #6a2310} {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}The {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #0000ff}We Will Chicago Plan{ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}, adopted by the 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Chicago Plan Commission in 2023, was Chicago’s first citywide plan in more than 50 years. Chicago doesn’t lack ambitious 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}aspirations for its future: We Will Chicago set out more than 40 goals and 150 objectives for the next ten years. But the Plan was not 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}designed as a “comprehensive” plan, meaning it does not offer specific land use reforms to help translate its goals into reality or 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}carry any statutory requirements for implementation. There is also no requirement for regular updates or review of the We Will 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Chicago Plan, which was tied to a mayoral administration, rather than an ongoing city strategy. Chicago’s last comprehensive 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}planning effort dates back to 1966.{ProximaNova-Regular 5.2 #000000} 22
[IMAGE 49,259,62,273]
[B 49,256,563,341]
  {ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #4f7a60}There is currently no mandate to comprehensively plan or regularly update zoning and land use policies as the city’s 
  {ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #4f7a60}needs change{ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #6a2310}. {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Instead, zoning changes often happen in response to specific development proposals—through rezonings for 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}individual projects or through the Planned Development process—and through localized corridor planning initiatives. Currently, 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}these changes require passage through City Council, which virtually always means deferring to the local alderperson. Without 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}citywide planning directives to steer development decisions, each ward fends for itself. Public input may occur during public 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}meetings of decision-making bodies, community meetings, and ward-level zoning advisory councils, but this is contingent on project 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}specifics and on each alder’s interest in—and approach to—public engagement.

  {ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #4f7a60}Local corridor planning is valuable, but limited in its reach. {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Between 2024 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}and 2026, DPD has worked with local alders on corridor-by-corridor zoning 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}changes, leading 16 rezoning efforts across 17 wards and encompassing at least 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}5,300 parcels. These initiatives are meaningful and show the benefits of moving 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}beyond a parcel-by-parcel approach to zoning. Still, corridor planning is valuable but 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}not sufficient: MPC estimates these rezonings cover less than 1% of total parcels in 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}the city. Without a comprehensive planning process and a citywide approach, it is 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}difficult if not impossible to make the changes we need at scale.

[B 49,454,563,539]
  {ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #4f7a60}Currently, zoning decisions ostensibly allow for public input, but a 
  {ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #4f7a60}multiplicity of meetings and lack of clarity makes for a complex and 
  {ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #4f7a60}inaccessible system.{ProximaNova-Regular 5.2 #000000}23{ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000} Because these meetings are subject to the Illinois Open 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Meetings Act, they are open to the public and must include an opportunity for public 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}comment. But that’s just about where the similarities end: whether public feedback actually matters for the decision itself, how the 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}meetings are scheduled and publicized, and how public comment works varies widely. Plus, other meetings that also have an 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}influence on zoning decisions, like ward-level zoning advisory councils, may or may not be subject to the Open Meetings Act.

[B 49,547,565,656]
  {ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #4f7a60}Without intentional and broad-reaching effort, public input often fails to be representative.{ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #6a2310} {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}There are persistent problems 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}with public feedback that need to be proactively addressed. A public meeting on an individual rezoning, for example, tends to 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}leave out some important groups: those who would like to weigh in but don’t have the resources; the unknown number of people 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}who may feel small-scale harm or benefit, which can collectively add up to a large impact; and any future residents of any 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}development enabled by the rezoning, who don’t live there yet. Research on public engagement patterns in Louisville showed that 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}public speakers in rezoning hearings are more likely to be residents from whiter and higher-income neighborhoods.{ProximaNova-Regular 5.2 #000000} 24{ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000} This tracks 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}with other research showing that opposition to development is more likely to come from older, white homeowners than others who 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}live in a community.{ProximaNova-Regular 5.2 #000000} 25{ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000} Engagement that is larger than a single-project scope can begin to address some of these shortcomings, 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}bringing in some of the voices that are missing when the conversation is limited to a single development.
