  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}multifamily housing development: 8% is zoned Residential Two-Flat, Townhouse, and Multi-Unit (RT), and 3% is zoned 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Residential Multi-Unit (RM).{ProximaNova-Regular 5.2 #000000} 7{ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000} This restrictive zoning policy leaves very little room to build new housing in large swaths of the city. This 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}also means that across substantial portions of the city, much of the housing that already exists, such as two-to-four flats, would be 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}illegal to build according to today’s rules. Significant portions of the city—the periphery of the city, as well as much of the Northwest 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}side—have fully utilized the current zoning envelope, meaning current zoning cannot accommodate any more housing units in those 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}areas. Other areas—the mid-South side and much of the near West side—do have room in the zoning envelope for housing 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}development.

  {ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #4e717a}Low-density residential zoning predominates even near transit. {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}About half of Chicagoans live within a half-mile of a CTA ‘L’ 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}station, and just 15% live within a quarter-mile, meaning they can get to a transit station by walking or using a mobility device in 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}5 minutes or less. One factor limiting the number of people who can live in transit-accessible housing is that a large amount of land 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}near transit (26% of land within a half-mile of an ‘L’ station) is still zoned to allow only low-density residential development (RS), 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}limiting access to both transit and housing. Citywide, a total of about 15 square miles of land is both within a half-mile radius of a CTA 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}rail station and zoned RS.
