  {ProximaNova-Regular 13.0 #4e5154}Understanding Planned Developments
[B 49,79,556,152]
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Some developments in Chicago go through a zoning process called the Planned Development process: a type of zoning 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}amendment that must pass the Plan Commission and City Council for a development to move forward. This process was designed 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}for large and complex projects, allowing developers and the City to directly negotiate and customize elements of development 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}the zoning code usually regulates directly, via a unique agreement that covers multiple buildings across a site. This includes land 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}uses, bulk and density, and open space or other public amenities that are included as part of a development. The PD process also 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}mandates that the developer hold at least one public meeting on the project. 
[B 49,160,563,209]
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}The PD process is a required step for some unusual types of developments on lots above a certain size, like hospitals, airports, 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}universities, and sports arenas. Depending on the zoning district, the process can also be required for buildings that are taller than a 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}designated height, or include more than a designated number of housing units, among other situations.{ProximaNova-Regular 5.2 #000000} 13{ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000} Some developments have 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}the choice of going through the PD process, or opting out.

  {ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #4e717a}PDs have become the most common zoning pathway for housing 
  {ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #4e717a}development in Chicago. {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}From 2003 to 2023, Chicago created more 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}than 600 PDs, together replacing by-right zoning with the Planned 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Development designation on an additional 5. 8 square miles of land. By 2023, 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}there were a total of about 1,400 PDs in Chicago. These PDs cover about 13% 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}of zoned land. In recent years, PDs have been the main pathway for adding 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}new housing units in the city. Urban Institute found that 58% of all new homes 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}added in Chicago between 2006 and 2024 were added through the PD 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}process, meaning each project was individually reviewed by the Plan 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Commission and City Council. In addition, most dense residential development 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}happened through the PD process: among new housing units added in 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}projects with five or more units, 68% were part of a PD.

  {ProximaNova-Regular 7.5 #4e5154}13 The number of housing units that triggers the PD process varies widely by zoning district—30 to 40 units in RT districts, 50 to 100 units in RM districts, 30 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 7.5 #4e5154}to 100 units in B and C districts, and 90 to 250 units in D districts. The height thresholds that trigger the PD process for residential development range from 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 7.5 #4e5154}75 feet (in C5 and B5) up to 600 feet in one of the densest D districts. While many cities use lot size thresholds to trigger a PD, Chicago is unique in having 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 7.5 #4e5154}triggers based on multiple factors: use, height or size by districts, and other factors such as air rights.
