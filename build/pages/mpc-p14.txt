  {ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #4e717a}Chicago’s zoning code limits the amount of new housing that can be legally built in the city. Though half the city is 
  {ProximaNova-Extrabld 9.0 #4e717a}zoned to allow homes, most of that land only allows very low density development. {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}53% of Chicago is set aside for homes 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}through residential zoning. Most of that land (41% of the city) is zoned Residential Single-Family (RS), a zoning district that allows the 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}construction of only single family homes, or two-flats in some limited circumstances.{ProximaNova-Regular 5.2 #000000} 6{ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000} This means 41% of the city is zoned to nearly 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}always allow only the most expensive type of housing: detached single family homes. 
[B 49,682,565,722]
  {ProximaNova-Regular 7.5 #4e5154}6 Under current zoning rules, two-flats are not permitted in the RS1 and RS2 zoning districts. In the remaining RS district, RS3, two-flats are allowed only under 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 7.5 #4e5154}specific circumstances. On a standard RS3 Chicago lot, a two-flat is allowed only when either (1) the lot is within the boundaries of the “Predominance of the 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 7.5 #4e5154}Block” district (a portion of the Northwest side), or (2) at least 60% of lots on the block contain multifamily housing. Because two-flats are allowed only in these 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 7.5 #4e5154}very limited cases, in this report we refer to RS districts as single-family districts.
