[B 58,469,529,506]
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000} Trees are powerful infrastructure, providing a host of benefits to neighborhoods: cleaner air, shade and cooling, greener 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}streets, and protection against growing climate risks. But addressing gaps in tree coverage is an ongoing challenge. One 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}element of this challenge: Chicago has a complex and fractured system of tree governance. 
[B 58,514,554,635]
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Many of Chicago’s trees sit in the parkway, the area between the sidewalk and the street. City law says that the City owns 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}these trees. However, a part of the zoning code called the Landscape Ordinance requires that developers undertaking certain 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}types of new construction or rehabilitation projects plant trees in the parkway. In those cases—usually residential development 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}of more than three units or parking lots above a certain size—developers must get approval for their planting plans from the 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Department of Planning and Development. To do the planting, developers must hire a contractor specifically licensed to work in 
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}the public way. Developers need clearance from the Department of Transportation to excavate, and a permit  
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}from Department of Streets and Sanitation’s Bureau of Forestry, which regulates trees in the parkway.  
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}After planting, the Landscape Ordinance gives the responsibility for routine maintenance of these  
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}required trees to property owners—except for tree trimming, removal, and other work affecting a tree’s  
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}health or structure, which is handled by the Bureau of Forestry. 
[B 58,643,429,704]
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}To further complicate the picture, plenty of trees are outside the parkway. Chicago Park District  
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}stewards trees on park land. Chicago Public Schools controls trees on school property.  
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}And trees on street medians, usually arterial streets medians or roundabouts, are the  
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}responsibility of the Chicago Department of Transportation (CDOT) or in some cases, the  
  {ProximaNova-Regular 9.0 #000000}Illinois Department of Transportation (IDOT).
