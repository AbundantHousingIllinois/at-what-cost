  {FuturaPTWeb-Light 17.8 #000000}ABOUT THE DATA
[B 46,73,547,87]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}This analysis combines parcel-level data from the Cook County Assessor, the City of Chicago, and our
[B 46,91,554,121]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}research partners at the Chicago Metropolitan Planning Council—including panel zoning data, building
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}permits, building footprints, and transit access—with census tract characteristics from the American
[B 46,124,144,138]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}Community Survey.
[B 46,152,565,166]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}To analyze housing development from 2011 to 2025, we examine parcels where residential development
[B 46,169,543,183]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}was allowed by right. We estimate each parcel’s zoned housing capacity and use a two-stage Poisson
[B 46,187,550,200]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}hurdle regression model. For parcels that underwent planned development reforms (i.e., a custom site
[B 46,204,565,218]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}plan that bypasses zoning requirements through negotiations with the city) in this timeframe, we assume
[B 46,221,553,235]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}that by-right development is equivalent to each planned development’s final underlying zoning district
[B 46,238,538,252]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}(as available). {Lato-Regular 11.5 #1696d2}Between 2006 and 2024, more than 60 percent of all multifamily development was in
[B 46,256,542,269]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #1696d2}planned developments. {Lato-Regular 8.6 #1696d2}(https://metroplanning.org/affordable-housing-outcome-part-2-of-2/){Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}Our approach is limited
[B 46,273,552,303]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}because we do not have data on all planned development projects and thus exclude about half of these
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}projects from our analysis. This also means a large share of the by-right permits we evaluate are on
[B 46,307,373,320]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}parcels that underwent planned development changes in the past.
[B 46,334,550,348]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}Using this data, we estimate the likelihood that a parcel received a housing permit based on its zoning,
[B 46,352,532,365]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}land value, and neighborhood characteristics. Then, we estimate the number of units permitted on
[B 46,369,558,383]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}parcels where development occurred. We applied these relationships to 2026 parcel and neighborhood
[B 46,386,560,400]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}conditions to construct a 2041 baseline and then model how permitting could change under four zoning
[B 46,403,566,417]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}reforms that would allow triplexes, four-to-nine-unit buildings, and higher-density housing near frequent
[B 46,421,551,434]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}transit. We assume that the baseline level of development in the next 15 years is the same as that over
[B 46,438,134,452]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}the past 15 years.
[B 46,465,530,479]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}For the ADU analysis, we link ADU permits issued from 2021 to 2025 to parcel and neighborhood
[B 46,482,544,496]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}characteristics and estimate a logistic regression for eligible parcels in Chicago’s pilot areas. We then
[B 46,499,539,513]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}sum parcel-level predicted probabilities and the geographic variation across pilot areas that did and
[B 46,517,545,530]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}didn’t require owner occupancy to estimate how many ADUs might have been permitted without the
[B 46,534,551,548]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}owner-occupancy requirements. Finally, we apply the model to eligible low-density residential parcels
[B 46,551,315,565]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}outside the pilot areas to simulate a citywide program.
[B 46,578,556,592]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}These scenarios illustrate how permitting might differ under alternative policies. They remain sensitive
[B 46,595,274,609]
  {Lato-Regular 11.5 #353535}to future market and demographic conditions.
